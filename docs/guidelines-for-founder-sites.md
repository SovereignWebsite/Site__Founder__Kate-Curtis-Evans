# Guidelines for founder and personal sites

Given by Dinis Cruz on 20 November 2026, after working through this site with Kate. Written down here so every new site like this one starts from them. They are public, like the site.

1. **Always show the designs.** Every site comes in five designs of the same facts, and a "Choose a design" control is on every page, so visitors and the owner can move from one to another. This matters most: what clicks for owners is seeing that a site can simply be made in all these designs. Then the only question is which one they prefer, and that is the question we want them asking.
2. **The agent may pick the home design.** Any of the five is fine; the owner can change it.
3. **The milestone is the handover.** The first design phase exists to hand the site to its owner in a state strong enough for them to carry on with their own Claude: adding images, changing words, choosing a design. Write the handover, the owner's prompt and the steps to connect their Claude to GitHub as part of the work.
4. **Ask the owner, not the operator.** Open questions (dates for figures, photo credits, which images may be used) go into a questions page for the owner, which their Claude works through with them.
5. **Do not push our requirements onto owners.** Agents sign their commits; owners do not have to.
6. **Sell the founder's story.** A founder site tells the story of an entrepreneur. The next generation of founders are specialists: people with deep expertise in one field who build the tool their field was missing. Tell that story (here: a practising legal transcriber who built Audio Genius), with one of the designs as a newsroom feature about it.
7. **Only what the owner has said.** Never invent a fact, figure, client or quote; date every figure; credit every photo.
