Below ground: the part nobody sees

Kate has stopped adding new features on purpose. What comes first is privacy and data protection, working through what the platform holds and how long it keeps it, one decision at a time. Because legal audio can hold highly sensitive information, she treats security, confidentiality and responsible data handling as fundamental, not features to bolt on later.

To be dug: the next stage

The space for a technical co-founder

Moving to a tech stack built for regulated data and getting certifications like Cyber Essentials in place. That is the work Kate wants a partner to own, ideally someone with legal tech or regulated-industry experience.

The platform Kate built Audio Genius on will not let Kate fork a test site from the live one, and gives her limited control over hosting and other parts of the build. She wants to own how it scales.

Built by someone who does the work

Kate has spent six years transcribing for legal professionals across the UK, and she still does the work every day. She kept seeing the same problem: most transcription technology is built for organisations processing audio, or for people capturing their own meetings. Very little of it is designed around the person producing the finished transcript from someone else's audio, with multiple speakers, difficult recordings, exacting standards, confidentiality requirements and deadline pressure.

So she built what she wanted to use herself, with no outside funding and no traditional tech background. She taught herself to build with vibe coding and AI tools, and learnt to test properly before anything goes live. It is being developed with feedback from real transcription professionals, and some of the best ideas for the platform have come directly from the people testing it.

“I'm not a tech founder guessing at what professional transcribers need. I am one, and that's the difference.”
Kate, on her LinkedIn profile
